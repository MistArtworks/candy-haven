Candy Haven dresses the broadcast now. OBSERVATORY gains a standing frame that sits over a capture for the whole stream — the first overlay in the kit that is furniture rather than an event.

## THE ENCLOSURE

A 1920×1080 frame for the whole broadcast. Gold registration brackets at the four corners, one obsidian plinth along the bottom carrying your marque, and a single crimson pip for when you are live.

Nothing runs along the edges, and that is the design rather than an omission. This is on screen for three hours at a stretch, so the frame has to say where the boundary is without spending it — roughly 96% of the canvas stays unobstructed, and the centre is fully transparent, so it dresses a capture rather than replacing one.

| Setting     | What it does                                                |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Marque**  | The name along the plinth. Uppercased, 32 characters         |
| **Section** | The numeral at the right of the plinth                       |
| **On air**  | Lights the crimson pip — the only colour on the whole frame  |

The pip is dark until you declare the broadcast live, so the frame carries no colour at all in its resting state.

## The address is the setting

THE ENCLOSURE is the only overlay in the kit that holds no live state. There is nothing to start, stop or push — it is settled entirely by the address it is loaded at, which is why its console page is a composer rather than a control panel.

Two consequences worth knowing. Changing a setting means pasting the new address into OBS rather than reloading the source. And two scenes can carry two enclosures with different marques at once, which a saved setting could not express.

Append `?guides=1` while you are cutting the scene and it outlines the inset the brackets sit on. The guide never appears on air.

## Under it

Because the frame reaches for nothing, its page forbids network access outright rather than opening the local event stream every other overlay needs. It costs a registry entry and two files instead of a service, a repository and a push.
