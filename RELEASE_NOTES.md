The register remembers when a record came out.

## Anniversaries

Every release that is **out** now marks its own anniversary on the CALENDAR. On
the same day in every year after it shipped, a hollow diamond appears saying how
long it has been — `MOVES LIKE JAGGAR · 3 YEARS`. Click it and the release
opens, exactly as the release date marker does.

It is drawn in all four lenses. MONTH and WEEK shorten the count to `3Y` where
the column is narrow; DAY gives it a line in the band above the day's entries;
AGENDA folds it into the ledger, so a day whose only business is an anniversary
still opens a row.

## Told apart from a release going out

The marker is deliberately the same object made quieter: a **hollow** diamond
against the filled one a release date draws, with the title a shade back. A day
that carries a record going out and the anniversary of an older one reads as two
different statements rather than as two releases — which it has to, at the size
a month cell gives it.

Gold, both of them. Crimson is reserved for what is live, and an anniversary is
the opposite of live: it is a date being remembered.

## Nothing is stored for it

The calendar has never kept a copy of a release date — it reads them from the
catalogue every time it draws. An anniversary is that same reading with the year
changed, which means there is nothing to keep in step: move a release date and
every anniversary moves with it, delete a release and they all go.

## The details

- Only **released** entries have one. A record still scheduled has not had its
  day yet.
- The count starts at **one year on**, so it never doubles up with the release's
  own marker on the original date.
- A record released on **29 February** is marked in leap years only. Printing it
  on the 28th would be recording a date it did not come out on.
- **AGENDA** lists one year ahead of whatever date it opens on — every record in
  the catalogue exactly once, rather than a ledger that never ends.

CATECHISM's CALENDAR chapter documents all of it.
