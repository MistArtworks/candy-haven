# World Aesthetic Brief — "Esoteric Retrofuturist Brutalism"

_Synthesized from all reference boards: Sacred Geometry, The Conduit, Monolithic Altar, The Observatory, Organic Brutalism, Brutalist Cybernoir, Dreamstate Y2K, Esoteric Retrofuturism, Red Sectum, Omega Core, Concrete Nexus, Monolith Ritual, the TVA-influenced master board, and the Sonoalchemy species sheets._

---

## Quick-paste summary (for a single generation prompt)

> Monumental brutalist cathedral architecture built to worship technology as religion. Vast concrete and marble monoliths, ribbed golden sacred-geometry portals, a single suspended crimson orb or vertical energy beam as the focal ritual object. Robed processions dwarfed by scale. Palette locked to obsidian black, aged concrete grey, brushed gold, crimson glass red, alabaster cream. Ancient and institutional at once, spiritual yet clinical, like an occult bureaucracy that has always existed and always will. ## 1. Theme — what this world is actually about

**The one-sentence version:** an occult bureaucracy outside of time, where technology is worshipped as religion and order is enforced as devotion.

**Core pillars (treat these as non-negotiable thematic anchors):**

- **Sacred Technology** — machines are altars, interfaces are relics. Nothing reads as "just" a device; every mechanism has liturgical weight.
- **Bureaucratic Divinity** — the ruling body is worshipped through _order_, not personality or idolization. Power is procedural, not charismatic.
- **Time as Infrastructure** — time itself is treated as something constructed, maintained, and policed, not merely experienced.
- **Clean Brutalism** — cold materials, warm accents. Function before form. Ritual expressed through repetition, not ornament for its own sake.
- **Harmony through Diversity** (from the species material) — all sentient life shares one deep biological origin ("resonance"); wildly different forms are compatible, not opposed. Unity is found in an underlying frequency, not in sameness.

**Six keyword anchors to check every asset against:** Control, Ritual, Harmony, Surveillance, Time, Obedience.

**Recurring location archetypes** (useful as scene prompts):

- **The Observatory** — cosmic observation and planetary surveillance.
- **The Archive** — endless halls of memory; records rewritten, realities curated.
- **The Council Chamber** — where decisions transcend generations; harmony decided for all, not by all.
- **The Processing Floor** — mortals reduced to data; choices measured, deviance erased.

**Tone one-liners pulled directly from the boards** (use these as caption/flavor-text style, or as a gut-check for whether a new piece fits):

- "A society built on order, where symmetry is worship."
- "The core remembers what the people have forgotten."
- "A machine of peace that erased its own history."
- "We do not question the shape of the universe."

## 2. Visual style — how it should actually look

**Scale and geometry:** Monumental, brutalist, symmetrical. Buildings are monolithic slabs, towering ribbed portals, concentric rings, or twin facing structures with a single suspended object between them. Human figures are always small against the architecture, this is deliberate: awe and hierarchy over individuality.

**The single-focal-object rule:** almost every board centers one glowing or suspended object as the ritual/technological heart of the space, a crimson sphere, a vertical energy beam, a concentric golden mandala portal, a pendulum-like orb on wires. New compositions should generally include one unmistakable focal object, not a cluttered scene.

**Material language:** veined marble and raw concrete as the "bones," aged/brushed gold or bronze as structural ribbing and ornament (often organic-looking, like grown filigree or dripping tendrils rather than machined trim), crimson glass or light as the one saturated accent, alabaster/cream stone for softer surfaces.

**Lighting:** low, warm, directional key light against cold stone, deep shadow, minimal fill. Light sources are usually diegetic and symbolic (a shaft through an oculus, a glow from the ritual object) rather than ambient.

**Figures:** robed, procession-like, uniform in silhouette (deep red or dark robes are the default "citizen/acolyte" costume). Individuality is subordinate to formation and symmetry.

**Genre range within the palette:** the boards span from oppressive/ritualistic (Monolithic Altar, Red Sectum) to clinical/bureaucratic (Brutalist Cybernoir, the TVA board) to one brighter outlier (Dreamstate Y2K, glossier and more retro-consumerist). All of them stay on-model through the same locked palette and monumental scale, that's the throughline even when mood shifts from oppressive to institutional to nostalgic.

## 3. Color and material palette (sampled directly from the reference boards)

| Material      | Hex       | Role                                                                      |
| ------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Obsidian      | `#0C0C0C` | base/background black, void, negative space                               |
| Concrete      | `#6F6656` | primary structural stone, warm grey                                       |
| Aged Brass    | `#5E472C` | secondary metal, darker bronze accents                                    |
| Brushed Gold  | `#976B30` | primary metal accent, ribbing, filigree, jewelry                          |
| Crimson Glass | `#43120F` | the one saturated color, always used sparingly, always at the focal point |
| Alabaster     | `#B69E7C` | warm cream stone, softer surfaces, robes trim                             |

Rule of thumb: this is a **five-color-max** world. Never introduce a hue outside this palette (no blues, greens, or bright primaries) unless deliberately breaking the pattern for a specific narrative reason.

## 4. Character / species design language (from the Sonoalchemy sheets)

- Elongated, elegant, elvish-alien humanoid proportions as the default body plan across variants.
- Each species reads as a clear adaptation to one extreme environment (deep ocean, high cave, upper atmosphere, arid desert, fungal network, crystal deposit, urban), but all share the same underlying "resonance" biology.
- All species carry visible gold linework on the skin, resonance nodes, like circuitry or ley-lines drawn directly onto flesh, not clothing.
- Adornment (jewelry, robes, filigree armor) is functional in-world, framed as literal instruments for tuning or perceiving resonance, not pure decoration.
- Keep the same five-color palette on character work: robes and skin tones stay within the concrete/alabaster/obsidian range, gold linework is the accent, crimson appears only at a single focal point (eyes, a gem, a mark).

## 5. Named touchstones (use as shorthand references for an agent or collaborator)

- **Loki's Time Variance Authority (TVA)** production design, the clearest and most explicit influence: bureaucratic halls filled with clerks, giant golden idol statues, brutalist concrete with warm brass and crimson banner accents.
- **Dune** — desert-mysticism-meets-empire scale and reverence for order.
- **Warhammer 40k** — gothic cathedral awe, oppressive monumental scale, devotion-through-architecture.
- **Mass Effect** (Asari-adjacent) — elegant alien elongation in character design.
- A streak of **biopunk** specifically for the "Organic Brutalism" variant (grown/dripping gold structures).

---

## 6. Explicit exclusions (what breaks the model)

- No bright primary colors, no saturated blue/green/purple.
- No cartoonish or chibi proportions.
- No clean minimalist "Apple-store" sci-fi, this world is monumental and textured, never sleek-and-empty.
- No medieval fantasy tropes played straight (castles, swords-and-sorcery iconography) without the sacred-technology / institutional twist.
- No cluttered compositions, every scene should still read as "one focal ritual object in a vast symmetrical space" even when busy with figures.
