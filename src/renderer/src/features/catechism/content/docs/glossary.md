# Glossary

This console names things in its own register. Most of the words mean exactly
what a plainer word would mean; a few carry a distinction worth knowing. Both
are listed, because the second kind is impossible to spot without the first.

## The console

**Department**: a section of the console, reached from the rail. Eighteen of
them, grouped into four divisions.

**Division**: the grouping a department is filed under: COMMAND, PRODUCTION,
PUBLICATION, OVERSIGHT. It tells you what kind of question the
departments inside it answer.

**Colophon**: the details the website carries: where to write, call and
message, who represents the artist and where they are, and the artist's profile
on every platform. Named for the note at the back of a book that says who made
it and where they can be found. Each field is **public** (goes out with the
website) or **private** (stays on this machine); a few are always public.

**Check-in**: CONTACT and SERVICES asking the website what is new: when the
console starts, when someone signs in, and when **Check for new** is pressed.
What comes back is kept as a copy in the archive, filed under the website it
came from.

**Waiting**: a message nobody has opened, or a DJ enquiry nobody has taken up.
Counted on the rail and in the notice the console gives when it starts.

**Reserved**: a department that is routed and specified but not yet built. Its
page lists what it will do when commissioned. INTERFACE and COLOPHON are reserved; COLOPHON's says it is coming soon.

**Masthead**: the header every page opens with: number, label, purpose, rule,
epigraph.

**Epigraph**: the flavour line under a masthead's rule. Drawn from the world
brief. It is never load-bearing; nothing depends on reading it.

**Panel**: the standard container. Square corners, hairline border, numbered
label.

**Focal**: the one crimson-accented element a view is allowed. At most one per
page, by rule.

**Readout**: a figure set in the monospace face. Numbers that should not shift
width as they change.

**Console**: the main window, with the rail and every department. One of the
windows Candy Haven shows, beside THE QUICK STRIP and the vestibule.

**Vestibule**: a small launcher with four doors: THE CONSOLE, NEW PROJECT,
ARCHIVE and OBSERVATORY. Opens at launch when REGULATION → STARTUP → **At
launch** says so, and from the strip.

**Tray**: the Candy Haven icon in the Windows notification area. Opens the
console, shows the strip again, reports the archive, checks for updates, and
quits. The console
**retires to the tray** when its close button is pressed: it is hidden, not
closed, and everything behind it keeps running.

## THE QUICK STRIP

**The Quick Strip**: the small window on top of everything, open from the
moment Haven starts. It holds the logo, Today, your pins, who is signed in, the
gear, **More** and the power button. It stands aside while the console is on
screen and comes back when the console is closed, retired to the tray or
minimised.

**Look**: how the strip is drawn. `PANEL`, a card with Today on top;
`HORIZONTAL`, one bar; `VERTICAL`, one column on a screen's edge. Set in
REGULATION → QUICK STRIP.

**Fold**: the strip put down to its logo alone, by clicking the logo. Clicking
it again opens the strip. It stays folded or open as you left it.

**Pin**: one button on the strip that opens one thing: a stack, a project, a
folder, a file, a department, a link or an action. Added by dropping a file, a
folder or a link on the strip, or from REGULATION → QUICK STRIP → **Pins**.

**Group**: one icon on the strip that drops down the pins in it. Up to 48 pins
each, one level deep: a group never holds another group.

**Action**: something the strip does itself rather than opens: **New
project**, **Add to today**, **Open a website**, **The console** and **The
vestibule**.

**On click**: how a stack or a project pin opens. A stack opens in ARCHIVE or
in Explorer; a project in Ableton, in ARCHIVE (its dossier) or as its folder.
Left on **Ask**, a click asks each time; **Always do this for this pin** stops
the asking.

**Today**: the strip's list for the day: follow-ups due by tonight, overdue
entries from the last month, today's CALENDAR entries, and releases out or
turning a year today. In `PANEL` it is the top row; in the other looks, the
today chip.

**Reminder**: a Windows notification about something due. A timed CALENDAR
entry has one a set number of minutes before it starts (REGULATION → QUICK
STRIP → **Reminders**, 10 by default). A follow-up has one when it comes due
(REGULATION → LEADS → **Remind me**). Clicked, it opens where you can act on it.

**More**: the strip's menu, behind the three dots: **Open a website…**, **Open
the console**, **Open the vestibule**, **Strip settings**, **Hide the strip**
and **Quit Candy Haven**.

**Hide the strip**: switches the strip off without quitting. **Show the Quick
Strip** on the tray icon, or **Show the strip** in REGULATION, brings it back.

**Power button**: the strip's own **Quit Candy Haven**, at the end of its
tools in every look. Not a pin: it can't be moved or removed. The first press
arms it; a second within three seconds quits Haven, archive and all.

## THE ARCHIVE

**The Archive**: both the department and the private database underneath it. In
REGULATION, "archive" settings are about the database; in the rail, ARCHIVE is
the department. To _archive_ a lead or an opportunity is a third thing again:
see LEADS below.

**Lens**: what is in scope. ALL, STACKS, INTAKE, BIN.

**View**: how whatever is in scope is drawn. ICONS, LIST, BOARD.

**The Stacks**: the filing tree you build, and the shelving in it. Named for the
shelving rather than for any one thing on it.

**Shelf**: a folder that holds projects. A genre or an artist, never a category.

**Category**: two different things, and this is the one genuine collision:

1. a **folder kind** at the top of the filing tree, which holds genres and artists;
2. a **project property** saying what kind of release it is (SINGLE, EP, ALBUM…).

Context separates them: a category you click into is a folder, a category on a
dossier is a property.

**Volume**: retired. An album, EP or compilation is now a **release** in
DISCOGRAPHY, which owns its own running order. A project's _category_ is still
only a label, and nothing validates it against the catalogue.

**Project**: one piece of work: a real directory holding a Live set, its
samples, bounces and backups. The unit everything in this department is about.

**Set**: the Ableton `.als` file itself. A project can hold several; one is
primary.

**Dossier**: the full record for one project, opened as an overlay.

**Stage**: where a project has got to in the pipeline. IDEA through RELEASED,
plus SHELVED off to the side. An opportunity in LEADS has a stage of its own,
which is a different scale.

**Pipeline**: the seven stages in a line. SHELVED is off-pipeline.

**Status**: where a _release_ has got to: DRAFT, SCHEDULED or RELEASED. A
different axis from a project's stage, which says how finished the work is.
RELEASED is never chosen: a release turns RELEASED on its release day by
itself.

**Intake**: bringing work that is already on disk into the register. Named for
what an archive does continually, rather than "migration", which is something a
database does once.

**Not indexed**: a directory the register has never seen. It cannot be filed
until a scan has indexed it.

**Filing root**: the directory your shelves live in. Chosen once, at the
ARCHIVE's setup gate, and reported in REGULATION; nothing in the department
works without it.

**Satellite root**: a further directory the scan also walks.

**Marked**: picked out for a bulk move, by tick or by Ctrl/Shift-click. Distinct
from **selected**, which is just where the cursor is.

**Final master**: the bounce that is the finished article, named in the
project's FINAL MASTER panel from TRACK READY on. Referenced where it already
sits in the project folder: choosing one moves nothing. The RELEASED stage
cannot be reached without it.

**Release master**: what a DISCOGRAPHY track says actually shipped. Copied from
the project's final master when the track is linked, then left alone, so an
already-released entry does not change its story when the project's pick does.

**Release Mastered Tracks**: a directory under the wrapper, left over from the
previous arrangement, into which a chosen master was moved. Nothing writes to it
now; a final chosen back then still points into it.

**The Bin**: deleted projects, in a real directory, kept until emptied.

## LEADS

ARTISTS' light record of the people you are building a relationship with, on
its **LEADS** and **OPPORTUNITIES** lenses beside the **ROSTER**. Everything in
it stays on this PC.

**Lead**: somebody you want to work with. A name, notes, profiles and links, an
email, a phone number and, when they are on it, a roster artist; with a
temperature, the interactions logged with them, and follow-ups. Not the same
as a roster entry: the roster is who the practice works with, a lead is who it
might.

**Temperature**: how warm a relationship is, from 0 to 100, drawn as an orb:
ice when frozen, ringed in gold at neutral, burning when hot, and white-hot at
supernova. Read in seven bands.

**Band**: one of the seven named stops on the temperature scale. A temperature
reads as the band nearest it.

| Band      | At  |
| --------- | --- |
| Frozen    | 0   |
| Cold      | 17  |
| Cool      | 33  |
| Neutral   | 50  |
| Warm      | 67  |
| Hot       | 83  |
| Supernova | 100 |

**Automatic**: a temperature worked out from the interactions and the cooling
since. Marked **From your interactions**. Every lead starts automatic, and
frozen.

**Set by you**: a temperature chosen by hand on the **Slider** or the
**Steps**. It holds, whatever is logged, until **Back to automatic**; a marker
on the slider shows where the interactions alone would put it.

**Interaction**: something that happened between you, logged with its points:
"They replied", "Met in person", or whatever you type. Removable if logged by
mistake.

**Predefined interaction**: one of the suggestions offered when logging, each
with its points. Kept in REGULATION → LEADS; up to 40.

**Points**: what an interaction moves a lead's temperature by, from −50 to +50.
An interaction keeps the points it was logged with, so changing a predefined
interaction later never re-scores the past.

**Cooling**: what a lead loses while nothing is logged: 10 points a quiet week
by default, counted to the day. Set in REGULATION → LEADS → **Cooling per quiet
week**; 0 stops it. The lead's history lists each quiet spell with what it
cost.

**Follow-up**: something to do about a lead or an opportunity, by a time: a
title, a due day and time, a priority (Low, Normal or High) and notes.

**First interaction**: the follow-up every new lead starts with. High
priority, due now unless you set another time, and with no reminder in the
sitting it was made, because you are looking right at it.

**Pending**: a follow-up not done whose time has not come.

**Overdue**: a follow-up not done whose time has passed. Shown in red
everywhere it appears.

**Complete**: a follow-up ticked off, by its tick or by logging an interaction
with **Also complete** ticked. A follow-up's status is always worked out from
its time and whether it is done; it is never set by hand.

**Snooze**: quiets a follow-up's reminders until **In an hour**, **This
evening** (18:00) or **Tomorrow morning** (10:00). It does not move the
follow-up: an overdue one stays overdue, and reminds again when the snooze
ends.

**Dismiss**: stops a follow-up's reminders for good, without completing it. It
stays open and marked **Reminders off**.

**Opportunity**: real work that came of a lead: a collaboration, a booking, a
remix, a paid project. Made from a lead's sheet with **Make an opportunity**;
the lead stays a lead. Its stage says how the work is going, which is a
separate thing from how warm the relationship is.

**Kind**: what an opportunity is: Collaboration, Booking, Remix, Paid project
or Other.

**Value**: what an opportunity is worth, when that is known, with its currency:
CAD unless you change it. OPPORTUNITIES adds up what is in play, per currency.

**Opportunity stage**: Open, In talks, Negotiating, Agreed and In progress,
then Won or Lost, which close it. An opportunity that is not won, lost or
archived is **live**.

**Archive** (a lead or an opportunity): put away without being removed. It
keeps its history, but its follow-ups raise no reminders and leave the strip,
NEXUS and CALENDAR, and it leaves the lists until **Archived** is shown.
Archiving a lead quiets its opportunities too. **Bring back from the archive**
returns a lead; an opportunity's **Archived** step returns it. Unrelated to the
ARCHIVE department.

## THE CALENDAR

**Entry**: one dated thing in the CALENDAR. Also one filing in a MUSTER roll;
context separates them.

**Day list**: a day's entries, releases and follow-ups in one sheet, opened by
clicking a date in MONTH. **Add an entry** files one on that day. In WEEK and
DAY, clicking an hour still files an entry directly.

**Follow-up mark**: a follow-up from LEADS as CALENDAR draws it, in all four
lenses: an arrow, then the time, the name and the task. Red when overdue,
struck through when done, and marked `!` when high priority. Read-only:
pressing it opens its lead or opportunity in ARTISTS.

## THE OBSERVATORY

**Overlay**: one browser source this console serves and drives.

**Browser source**: OBS's term for a web page composited into a scene. Every
overlay is one.

**Canvas**: the width and height an overlay is designed for, quoted beside its
address on the bench.

**Full / panel**: whether an overlay is its own scene, or furniture composited
over one.

**Address**: the URL an overlay answers on. Some overlays answer on more than
one, each pinning a different presentation of the same state.

**Citizen**: a viewer in chat. Used wherever chat participation is counted.

**The Muster**: an open call. Chat files entries against a question.

**Roll**: the numbered list of entries a muster collects.

**Resonance Selection**: the weighted draw. Also called _the rite_.

**Petition**: one entry in a draw.

**Weight**: how much of the field a petition occupies. 1 to 999.

**Mechanism**: how a draw is presented: RESONANCE RING, THE PROCESSION, THE
DESCENT.

**Elimination**: withdrawing each winner from the pool, so a series of draws
walks the field.

**The Concord**: the vote. Also called _the chamber_ while it sits.

**Ballot**: the numbered options chat votes between.

**The Casting**: what settles a deadlocked Concord. Equal lots, one taken.

**Now Transmitting**: live Spotify playback.

**The Chorus**: chat as an institutional register. Pasted into Streamlabs
rather than served from here.

**Interval / Convening**: the two countdowns. INTERVAL is for breaks; CONVENING
opens a broadcast.

**Grace**: time counted past zero, in crimson, rather than stopping.

**Terminal word**: what CONVENING resolves to at zero. `TIME` by default.

**Face**: how a countdown is drawn. Five of them.

**Rehearsal**: test mode, for exercising the kit with no audience.

## THE LORE

**Draft**: a chapter as written in LORE. Kept on this PC only; the other copy
of the console never sees it. Not the same as a release's DRAFT status, which
is a release not yet announced.

**Publish**: sending one chapter to the website as it stands, with a copy of
its planet. The only way anything written in LORE reaches the website.

**Live**: the website showing the lore published from here rather than its
own files. Set by the first publish, for good. A live opportunity, in LEADS, is
only one still being worked.

**Chapter address**: the end of a chapter's link, `/lore/the-planet`. Fixed
while the chapter is published. Not the same as an overlay's address.

**Preset**: one of the eleven planets the lore began with. Never changed;
duplicated to make a planet of your own.

**Layer**: one part of a planet: one of 26 kinds, with its own colours, shape,
place and motion. Up to 24, stacked.

## Elsewhere

**Boot stage**: one of the nine steps the launch sequence performs. Named in
the NEXUS boot report.

**Harmonics**: the final boot stage: a health check of the archive.

**Follow-ups card**: the small card on the NEXUS landing, at the top right
under SONOALCHEMY, listing the follow-ups due by tonight. Overdue ones are in
crimson, with a pulse. **–** folds it to a mark; a follow-up that was not
there when you folded it brings the card back.

**The Board**: the shared database behind DISPATCH. Not this machine's.

**Operator**: you. Used wherever the console means the person using it, as
opposed to a viewer.

**Item**: one entry on the DISPATCH board.

**Ruling**: settling an item as RESOLVED or DENIED, with a reason.

**Accent**: the interactive colour, crimson or gold. Set in REGULATION.

**Grain**: the film texture over the whole console. Adjustable to zero.
