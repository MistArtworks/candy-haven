# COLOPHON

The details the website carries: where to write, call, message and follow.

## What it is

COLOPHON is the first department of **PUBLICATION**, the division for what the
public reads when nothing is live. BROADCAST is what an audience sees while a
stream is on; PUBLICATION is the standing record they can read at any hour,
which is the website and everything it carries.

A colophon is the note at the back of a book saying who made it and where they
can be found. The website's footer and its contact page are exactly that note,
so this is where it is written: once, here, rather than typed into the site.

It is one record, not a register. Every detail and every platform has exactly
one field, and all of them are on the page, filled or not. There is nothing to
add or remove; a field left empty is a detail not known yet.

## Correspondence

Where people write, call and message.

| Field                 | What it is                                    | The rule                                                                   |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Booking email**     | Where bookings and questions are written to   | A name, an `@` and a domain                                                |
| **Management email**  | For labels, brands and partnerships           | The same                                                                   |
| **Press email**       | For press, interviews and features            | The same                                                                   |
| **Phone number**      | The number, as it should read on the page     | Digits and `+ ( ) - .` only; a `+` only at the start; 7 to 15 digits       |
| **WhatsApp number**   | The WhatsApp line, which may be another phone | A phone number that starts with `+` and its country code                   |
| **Discord username**  | The username people message after a booking   | 2 to 32 characters: lowercase letters, numbers, `.` and `_`; no `..`       |
| **Telegram username** | The Telegram account                          | 5 to 32 characters: letters, numbers and `_`; starts with a letter; no `@` |

A phone number is **kept as you space it**: `+1 (902) 555-0142` is stored as
typed, because how a number reads is a local custom and yours to choose. What
it dials is taken from its digits, keeping the `+`, and shown under the field
as `Dials as +19025550142` so you can see exactly what a phone will be handed.

A WhatsApp number must carry its country code, because a WhatsApp link is
opened from anywhere in the world and has no local area to assume. Under the
field it shows the chat it opens, `Opens at wa.me/19025550142`, and **Open**
beside its label tries it. A Telegram username shows `Opens at t.me/…` the same
way.

A Discord username is refused rather than corrected. Typing `CandyHeist` is
told that usernames are lowercase; it is not quietly stored as `candyheist`,
because a record should hold what you wrote. The old `name#1234` tags are
refused with the reason, since Discord retired them. Telegram matches
usernames without regard to case, so there either case is fine.

## Particulars

Who represents the artist, and where they are.

| Field              | What it is                                    | The rule                      |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Based in**       | City and country, as the website prints them  | 64 characters at most         |
| **Time zone**      | The zone the artist works in                  | A zone from the zone database |
| **Management**     | Who manages the artist, a person or a company | 96 characters at most         |
| **Booking agency** | The agency that books the shows               | 96 characters at most         |
| **Label**          | The label the records come out on             | 96 characters at most         |
| **Press kit**      | The electronic press kit                      | Any `http` or `https` address |

The time zone is written as the zone database writes it, `Europe/London` or
`America/Halifax`, not as an offset: an offset is wrong for half the year
anywhere that keeps summer time. Once it is set, the field shows what time it
is there now. **Use local** fills in this machine's own zone. Only a wrong case
is refused rather than corrected, by the same rule as a Discord username.

Leave **Management** and **Label** empty while the artist manages themselves
and releases independently.

## Profiles

The artist's profile on every platform the colophon knows, one field per
platform, never two, because the website draws each platform behind its own
mark and has nowhere to put a second. They are set out in seven panels.

**Essentials.** Instagram, Spotify, Apple Music, SoundCloud and YouTube. The
navbar and the footer draw Instagram, SoundCloud, Spotify and YouTube, and say
so under their fields. YouTube here is the channel; YouTube Music is under
Listen.

**Listen.** YouTube Music, Amazon Music, Deezer, TIDAL, Pandora, iHeartRadio,
Qobuz, Napster, Audiomack, Audius, Shazam, Anghami, Boomplay, JioSaavn, Gaana,
Wynk Music, NetEase Cloud Music, QQ Music, KKBOX, Melon, JOOX, LINE MUSIC and
Yandex Music.

**Follow.** TikTok, X, Facebook, Threads, Bluesky, Snapchat, Reddit, Tumblr,
Pinterest, LinkedIn, VK, Weibo and Linktree.

**Live, mixes and video.** Twitch, Kick, Vimeo, Mixcloud, hearthis.at,
1001Tracklists, Resident Advisor, Songkick and Bandsintown.

**Stores and services.** Bandcamp, Beatport, Beatsource, Traxsource, Juno
Download, 7digital, BeatStars, Airbit and SoundBetter.

**Community and support.** Discord server, Telegram channel, WhatsApp channel,
Patreon, Ko-fi and Buy Me a Coffee.

**Catalogues.** Discogs, MusicBrainz, Genius, Musixmatch, Last.fm, AllMusic and
Wikipedia.

Seventy-two in all. Each panel counts how many of its platforms are set, and
the Publication panel counts them all.

An address must be on the platform its field is for. A SoundCloud address
pasted into Spotify's field is refused as `That address is on SoundCloud, not
Spotify`, because the website would draw it behind the wrong mark and nobody
would notice until somebody clicked it. Amazon Music and Yandex Music are
recognised on every country's domain. Once an address is accepted, **Open**
beside the platform's name opens it in the browser.

> These are **not** your card on the roster. The card is every address you can
> be found at, kept so credits can point somewhere. These are the ones the
> website points people to. Tying the two together would put a page you added
> for a credit into the website's footer.

## Filing

The page is a draft. Typing changes nothing in the archive; the unsaved-changes
bar rises at the bottom of the console, and **File changes** writes every
changed field at once. **Discard** forgets every unfiled edit and shows the
record as it was last filed.

This is the way REGULATION files settings, and it is chosen for the same
reason. The roster's sheet saves as you stop typing, which suits a record you
are reading as much as writing. This one is built into a website, and an
address saved halfway through being typed would be published halfway through.

While anything is unfiled, picking another department on the rail nudges the
bar instead of navigating. File it or discard it first.

> The guard is on the rail, as it is for REGULATION. A keyboard chord or the
> command palette still leaves the page, and an unfiled change goes with it.

If a field is red when you file, nothing is written and the bar says so. The
archive applies the same rules again when the record arrives, and checks every
field before it writes any, so a colophon can never hold an address that is
not one, whatever writes it.

## Where it is kept

In the archive, on this machine, in the `publication_colophon` collection, as
one document. It is archive data rather than a setting, so REGULATION's export
does not carry it, and it survives a settings reset untouched.

Nothing reads it from outside yet. The **Publication** panel at the foot of the
page says exactly that: kept here, not yet published, how many profiles are
set, and when it was last filed. Publishing the website from this console is
still to come, and this is the first record it will be built from.

A colophon filed while this page still kept a list of links is read into the
profiles: each link goes under the platform its address is on, and one on no
platform the colophon knows is left out.
