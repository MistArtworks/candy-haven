# LEADS

The people you are building a relationship with, and the work that comes of
them: a light way to keep track of who you've reached out to, how warm it is,
and what to do next.

![leads-01-list.png](leads-01-list.png)

## Where it is

LEADS is a department of its own, the first in **COMMUNITY**, beside ARTISTS.
Until 1.27.0 it was two lenses inside ARTISTS; an old address or strip pin to
those lenses still lands here. The rail under its heading has two lenses:

| Lens              | Shows                                                      |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **LEADS**         | The people you're getting to know, warmest to coldest      |
| **OPPORTUNITIES** | The concrete work that came of a lead, as a board by stage |

Each lens shows its count. LEADS also says how many follow-ups are overdue, in
crimson, so you can see it from OPPORTUNITIES without switching.

The lens, and any open lead or opportunity, is part of the address. A reminder,
the quick strip, NEXUS and CALENDAR all use that to land you on the right
record. ARTISTS is still where the roster lives: a lead linked to somebody on
it says **On the roster as** their name, and that opens their record there.

## What a lead is

A lead is somebody you want to work with but don't yet: a singer you found
through a cover, a producer whose sound would suit a set, a booker you met at a
show. Each one has:

| Part              | What it holds                                                   |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**          | How you know them                                               |
| **Temperature**   | How warm the relationship is, from frozen to supernova          |
| **Interactions**  | What has happened between you, each with its points             |
| **Follow-ups**    | What to do next, and when                                       |
| **Contact**       | Profiles and links, email, phone and notes                      |
| **On the roster** | The roster record, when they're already an artist you work with |

A lead is not a roster entry. The roster is who you work with; a lead is who
you're getting to know. When somebody is both, link the two (see **Editing a
lead**) and the lead says **On the roster as** their name.

Leads are kept in the archive on this PC, like everything else, and nothing
about them goes to the website.

## The LEADS list

The list leads with the follow-ups that need you, then everybody, each with
their orb.

![leads-02-due.png](leads-02-due.png)

**FOLLOW-UPS DUE** gathers every follow-up that is overdue or due today,
across all leads and their opportunities, oldest first. The panel turns crimson
while anything in it is overdue. Each row names whose it is: press the name to
open that lead or opportunity. Tick, **Snooze**, **Dismiss** and **Edit** work
here exactly as they do on the lead itself.

Below it, each lead is one row:

| Column          | Shows                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The orb         | Their temperature, drawn. See **Temperature**                            |
| Name and handle | Their name, and their first profile as `@handle · Platform`              |
| Tags            | **On the roster** and how many open opportunities they have, if any      |
| Temperature     | The percentage and its band, and **Set by you** when pinned by hand      |
| Next            | The follow-up most worth doing next, and when it's due or how late it is |
| Last            | The last thing logged, and how long ago                                  |

Click a row to open the lead.

The bar above the list:

- **Search** matches the name, any profile link and the email address.
- **Show** chooses **Active** (everybody not archived), **Overdue** (only leads
  with a follow-up overdue) or **Archived**.
- **Sort** chooses **Needs you** (overdue first, then the soonest due, then the
  warmest), **Hottest** or **Newest**.

## How to add a lead

1. On LEADS, press **Add a lead**.

   ![leads-12-add.png](leads-12-add.png)

2. Type their **Name**. That is the only thing needed.
3. Add what you have: **Where you found them** (a profile or page link),
   **Email**, **Phone**, **Notes**, and **On the roster** if they're already an
   artist you work with.
4. **First interaction due** is when you mean to reach out. It is _now_ unless
   you pick a day and time. The quick picks set it in one press: **Now**, **In
   an hour**, **This evening**, **Tomorrow 10:00**, **In a week**.
5. Press **Add**. Their sheet opens.

Every new lead comes with its first follow-up, **First interaction**, at high
priority, due when you said. It starts frozen, at 0%: nothing has happened yet.

> A first interaction due now doesn't send a reminder while you're looking at
> it. It sits in **FOLLOW-UPS DUE**, on the strip and in NEXUS as overdue until
> it's done, and the next time Haven opens it is part of the overdue count.

## A lead's sheet

![leads-03-sheet.png](leads-03-sheet.png)

The sheet opens on what you came for. Across the top: the orb, the name, the
temperature in its own colour with its band, and whether it comes **From your
interactions** or is **Set by you**. Beneath that, when they were added, how
many interactions there have been and when the last was, and **On the roster
as** their roster name, which opens their roster record.

The left column is the relationship: **TEMPERATURE**, **LOG AN INTERACTION** and
**INTERACTIONS**. The right column is what to do: **FOLLOW-UPS**,
**OPPORTUNITIES** and **CONTACT**.

**Edit** opens their details; **Close** or **Esc** puts the sheet away. An
archived lead can't be logged against, re-tempered or given new follow-ups
until it's brought back; its details can still be edited.

## Temperature

How warm the relationship is, from 0 to 100, drawn as an orb that freezes over
when it's cold and burns when it's hot. Seven named stops mark the scale:

| Band          | At  | Reads as                               |
| ------------- | --- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Frozen**    | 0   | Nothing has happened, or it went quiet |
| **Cold**      | 17  | A little contact, a while ago          |
| **Cool**      | 33  | Some back and forth                    |
| **Neutral**   | 50  | A real conversation is going           |
| **Warm**      | 67  | Regular, friendly contact              |
| **Hot**       | 83  | Working towards something together     |
| **Supernova** | 100 | As good as it gets                     |

A lead's band is the stop nearest its temperature: 73% reads **Warm**.

### From your interactions

By default the temperature works itself out from what you log:

- Every interaction adds its own **points**, between −50 and +50. A reply might
  be +15, a call +25, an unfollow −20.
- Every **quiet week** cools it, by 10 points a week unless REGULATION says
  otherwise, worked out to the day: three quiet days cost about 4.
- It never goes below 0 or above 100.

So a lead you met in person (+30) and who replied a week later (+15) sits at
about 35, Cool. Leave it a fortnight and it cools to about 15, Cold.

An interaction keeps the points it was logged with. Changing a suggestion's
points in REGULATION counts from then on, and never re-scores the past.

### Setting it by hand

Sometimes you know better than the log. Set it yourself, two ways:

![leads-04-slider.png](leads-04-slider.png)

- **Slider**: drag the thick bar, or press a named stop under it. The orb
  follows as you drag, and the value is saved when you let go.
- **Steps**: one press per named stop, like the stage strip in an ARCHIVE
  dossier.

![leads-05-steps.png](leads-05-steps.png)

The switch in the panel's corner chooses between them, and Haven remembers
which you used last.

Set by hand, the temperature **holds**: logging and quiet weeks no longer move
it. The chip says **Set by you**, and a small marker on the slider shows where
the interactions alone would put it.

![leads-06-pinned.png](leads-06-pinned.png)

Press **Back to automatic** to let the interactions decide again. Hover it to
see the value it will go back to.

## Logging an interaction

Every time something happens between you, log it: a reply, a message, a call, a
comment, a meeting. That's what keeps the temperature honest and the history
useful.

![leads-07-log.png](leads-07-log.png)

### How to log one

1. Type what happened in **What happened?**, in your own words.
2. Set its **points** in the box beside it: positive for good news, negative for
   bad. It starts at 5.
3. Press **Enter**, or **Log**.

The suggestions under the row are the predefined interactions from REGULATION.
Press one to fill in both the words and its points, then Enter. Typing a
suggestion's name in full brings its points with it too, unless you've already
typed points of your own. As you type, the suggestions narrow to the ones that
match.

| Suggestion                   | Points |
| ---------------------------- | ------ |
| They replied                 | +15    |
| They messaged first          | +18    |
| Candy commented              | +6     |
| Candy replied to their story | +5     |
| Call or video call           | +25    |
| Met in person                | +30    |
| They unfollowed              | −20    |

These are the defaults. REGULATION, under LEADS, renames them, changes their
points, removes them and adds your own.

**Also complete** names the lead's most pressing follow-up. Leave it ticked and
logging also marks that follow-up done. It's ticked by default when that
follow-up is overdue, because answering it is usually why you're logging.

### The history

![leads-08-history.png](leads-08-history.png)

**INTERACTIONS** lists everything that has happened, newest first, down to
**Added as a lead** at the bottom. Each line shows its points and how long ago
it was; hover the time for the exact date.

Between them, **Quiet for N weeks** lines show what a quiet spell cost, so the
number on the orb can always be added up from what is on screen. They are
worked out, not stored: change the cooling in REGULATION and they change with
it. A spell that cost nothing, because the lead was already frozen, isn't
shown.

Logged something by mistake? Press the faint **×** at the end of its line to
delete it. The temperature is worked out again without it.

## Follow-ups

A follow-up is the next thing to do about a lead: reply to them, send the
stems, agree the split. Each has a title, a due day and time, a priority, and
notes if you want them.

Its status is worked out from the clock, never set by hand:

| Status       | When                                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **PENDING**  | Due later                                             |
| **OVERDUE**  | Its time has come and it isn't done. Drawn in crimson |
| **COMPLETE** | Ticked off                                            |

High-priority follow-ups carry a **HIGH** mark.

### How to add a follow-up

1. On the lead (or an opportunity), press **Add a follow-up**.

   ![leads-10-task.png](leads-10-task.png)

2. Name it in **Follow-up**: what to do.
3. Set **Due**: a day and a time, typed as `14:30`, or one of the quick picks.
   It starts at tomorrow, 10:00.
4. Choose a **Priority**: **Low**, **Normal** or **High**.
5. Add **Notes** if there's something to remember, then press **Add the
   follow-up**.

### Doing something about one

![leads-09-snooze.png](leads-09-snooze.png)

| To                 | Do this                                                                                   |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mark it done       | Tick its square. Tick again to undo                                                       |
| Put it off         | **Snooze**, then **In an hour**, **This evening** (18:00) or **Tomorrow morning** (10:00) |
| Stop its reminders | **Dismiss**. It stays open and overdue, just quiet                                        |
| Change it          | **Edit**: its title, due time, priority or notes, then **Save**                           |
| Delete it          | **Delete**, beside Edit, then **Delete** again to confirm. Or **Edit**, then **Delete**   |

Snooze and Dismiss appear once a follow-up is overdue: before that there is
nothing to quiet. A snoozed follow-up says **Snoozed to** and when; a dismissed
one says **Reminders off**.

> Snoozing quiets the reminder; it doesn't move the due time, so the
> follow-up still reads overdue. To actually move it, **Edit** its due time.
> A new due time clears any snooze or dismissal, so the new time reminds you
> afresh.

Done follow-ups fold away under **Show N done**.

### Where follow-ups show

The same follow-ups appear everywhere they're useful, and doing something about
one in any of these places does it everywhere:

- **LEADS**: **FOLLOW-UPS DUE** at the top of the list, and each lead's own.
- **The quick strip**: overdue ones lead its Today row in crimson, and its
  Today list starts with every follow-up due today, with **Log and complete**,
  **Snooze** and **Dismiss** in reach. See the QUICK STRIP chapter.
- **NEXUS**: a small card under SONOALCHEMY lists what's due. See NEXUS.
- **CALENDAR**: each follow-up is marked on its day, at its time. See CALENDAR.
- **Windows notifications**: see **Reminders** below.

## Reminders

With **Remind me** on in REGULATION, under LEADS (it is, by default):

- **When Haven opens**, one notification says how many follow-ups are overdue,
  or names the one if there's only one.
- **As each follow-up comes due**, it gets a notification of its own: who, what,
  and when it was due. More than two coming due at once (after the PC wakes,
  say) arrive as one notification.
- **A snooze** reminds you again when it ends.
- **Dismissed** follow-ups don't remind, and **done** ones are gone from the
  list altogether.

Click a reminder and it opens what it's about. With the console on screen, that
is the lead (or opportunity) in LEADS. Otherwise the follow-up opens beside
the quick strip, with **Log and complete**, **Open the lead**, **Snooze** and
**Dismiss**, so most of them never need the console.

A reminder only comes while Haven is running. If Windows is set to **Do not
disturb** or Focus, notifications wait in the notification centre instead.

## Editing a lead

![leads-11-edit.png](leads-11-edit.png)

Press **Edit** on the sheet. Everything saves as you stop typing; there is no
save button. **Done editing** goes back to the sheet.

| Field                  | For                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**               | How you know them. It can't be empty                                          |
| **On the roster**      | Their roster record, when they're already an artist you work with, or none    |
| **Email**, **Phone**   | How to reach them. Shown under CONTACT, selectable to copy                    |
| **Profiles and links** | Instagram, TikTok, SoundCloud, a website: paste the address and press **Add** |
| **Notes**              | How you came across them, what they make, who you know in common              |

Links work as they do on the roster: the platform is guessed from the address,
**Open** opens it in your browser, **Remove** takes it off. On the sheet they're
listed under CONTACT, one press from opening.

## Archiving and deleting a lead

![leads-17-delete.png](leads-17-delete.png)

At the foot of every lead's sheet:

- **Archive** puts a lead away when it's gone nowhere, keeping everything. It
  leaves the list (find it under **Show: Archived**), its follow-ups stop
  appearing anywhere and stop reminding, and its sheet becomes read-only.
  **Bring back from the archive** undoes it.
- **Delete lead**, in red, deletes the lead for good: its history, its
  follow-ups, and every opportunity that came of it. The sheet asks first, and
  says how many opportunities go with it; press **Delete** to confirm or
  **Keep** to change your mind. There is no undo, which is what Archive is for.

## OPPORTUNITIES

An opportunity is something concrete that came of a lead: a collaboration, a
booking, a remix, a paid project. It has its own stage, value, notes and
follow-ups.

Stage and temperature are different things, kept apart on purpose. The
temperature describes the relationship; the stage describes the work. A hot
relationship can carry a stalled project, and a cool one can still close a
booking.

### How to make an opportunity

1. Open the lead and press **Make an opportunity**, under OPPORTUNITIES.

   ![leads-13-convert.png](leads-13-convert.png)

2. Say **What it is**, in a line: `Remix of Afterglow`.
3. Choose its **Kind**: **Collaboration**, **Booking**, **Remix**, **Paid
   project** or **Other**.
4. Add its **Value** if there is one, as a number (`1200` or `1200.50`), and its
   **Currency** (CAD unless you change it). Both are optional.
5. Add **Notes** about terms, dates or who else is involved, then press **Make
   it**.

The opportunity starts at **Open**. A notice offers **Open it**.

The lead isn't used up: it stays on LEADS with its contacts, history and
temperature, and can carry more opportunities over time. Its sheet lists them
all under OPPORTUNITIES, each with its kind, value and stage; press one to open
it.

### The board

![leads-14-board.png](leads-14-board.png)

OPPORTUNITIES lays every opportunity out by stage, one column each, with its
count. Each card shows its kind, title and value, its lead's name beside a small
orb with the lead's temperature, and its next follow-up. Press a card to open it.

The bar above says how many are **live** (not won, lost or archived) and how
much is **in play**: the values of the live ones added up, per currency.
**Search** matches the title and the lead's name. **Archived** shows the
archived ones instead.

### An opportunity's sheet

![leads-15-opportunity.png](leads-15-opportunity.png)

Across the top: its kind and value, its title, when it was made, and when it was
won or lost. On the right, its **LEAD**: the orb, name and temperature. Press it
to open the lead.

Below that, the **STAGE** strip, then **FOLLOW-UPS** on the left and
**DETAILS** on the right: what it is, its kind, its value and currency, and
notes. Details save as you go; the value saves when you press Enter or leave the
field.

### Stages

![leads-16-stages.png](leads-16-stages.png)

| Stage           | Means                                               |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Open**        | Something to pursue. Where every opportunity starts |
| **In talks**    | You're discussing it                                |
| **Negotiating** | Terms, fees or credits are being worked out         |
| **Agreed**      | Both sides have said yes                            |
| **In progress** | The work is happening                               |
| **Won**         | Done and delivered, or signed and paid              |
| **Lost**        | It isn't happening                                  |

Press any stage to move it there, in any order: a deal can go straight from In
talks to Agreed. Stages it has passed are underlined in brass, and the current
one in crimson.

**Won** and **Lost** sit apart from the run, because they close it: the sheet
records when. Moving it back to an earlier stage reopens it.

**Archive**, set apart again, keeps an opportunity without it being in the way.
It leaves the board (find it under **Archived**), its follow-ups stop showing
and reminding, and its stages lock until you press **Archived** again to bring
it back.

### Deleting an opportunity

**Delete opportunity**, in red at the foot of its sheet, then **Delete** to
confirm, deletes it and its follow-ups for good. The lead stays as it was.
Archiving keeps everything, if you might want it later.

## Settings

REGULATION, under LEADS, holds the predefined interactions and their points,
how much a quiet week cools a lead, and whether follow-ups remind you. See
REGULATION.

![regulation-08-leads.png](regulation-08-leads.png)

> LEADS lives in the archive like the rest of the console, on this PC only. If
> the archive isn't connected, LEADS says so instead of showing an empty list,
> and nothing can be added until it is back. NEXUS shows why.
