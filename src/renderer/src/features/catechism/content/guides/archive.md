# ARCHIVE

## Lenses decide what is in scope

![archive-01-lenses.png](archive-01-lenses.png)

The rail down the left is the lens. It changes _what you are looking at_, not
how it is drawn.

- **STACKS** — the filing tree you build. Categories, genres and artists, and the
  projects shelved on them. These are real directories on disk.
- **INTAKE** — work found elsewhere on disk that is not in the register yet.
- **VOLUMES** — albums, EPs and compilations, and the tracks bound into each.
- **ALL** — the whole register, flat.
- **BIN** — deleted projects, kept until you empty them.

## Views decide how it is drawn

![archive-02-board.png](archive-02-board.png)

The toggle beside the panel switches between three presentations of whatever the
lens has in scope.

- **PLATES** — a grid of objects. The default, because a shelf is browsed before
  it is audited.
- **LEDGER** — the table, when the figures are the point.
- **BOARD** — the pipeline, in columns. Drag a project between columns to change
  its stage; the move _is_ the edit.

## The pipeline

Six stages, in order, plus one off to the side.

`IDEA` → `SKETCH` → `ARRANGEMENT` → `MIX` → `MASTER` → `TRACK READY`

`SHELVED` is off-pipeline — parked indefinitely, kept for parts rather than for
release. A project cannot reach TRACK READY until you have named which file
actually ships.

## INTAKE files what is already on disk

![archive-03-intake.png](archive-03-intake.png)

Point INTAKE at a folder and it lists what it finds that the register does not
know about. Pick a shelf, move the work onto it, and it becomes a project.

> Whether intake moves or copies is set in REGULATION, under ARCHIVE. Moving is
> the default, because two copies of a project is how you end up mixing the
> wrong one.

## The dossier

![archive-04-dossier.png](archive-04-dossier.png)

Open a project and you get its record: overview, its files, its mixes and
masters, and the history of every stage it has passed through.

Marking a mixdown and a master here is what lets the project reach TRACK READY —
the stage is a claim about a specific file, not a mood.
